--- PAGE 12 ---
What prevents simulator-ready terrain? 
Current blockers
● Camera baseline is 242.059 calibration units, but the archive does not define the physical unit (most 
likely mm, but not guaranteed)
● Left and right images were taken about 30–43 seconds apart
● Rover motion during that interval is not documented
● Per-image pose and gravity orientation are unavailable
● Shadows and low-texture areas leave real holes in the terrain
● No surveyed ground truth exists for absolute accuracy validation
What is still needed
1. Confirm the CAHV camera-center units and coordinate frame
2. Obtain stationarity confirmation or motion telemetry
3. Obtain complete per-image metadata and attitude information
4. Validate scale and error against a known target or trusted DEM
5. Convert the verified result into gravity-aligned, collision-ready simulator terrain

Stereo Depth Status & Blockers
● Missing Camera Intrinsics
○ Missing official camera intrinsics, distortion values, and CAHV vectors. Cannot yet convert 
displacement to metric depth.
● Capture-Time Gap
○ Candidate left/right images are 30–43s apart without confirmation that the rover remained 
stationary between shots.
● Current Capabilities
○ Currently able to derive feature correspondences, epipolar geometry, and relative 
displacement.
● Pending Metrics
○ Not yet able to extract accurate metric depth, rock dimensions, rocks per square meter, metric 
spacing, or slopes.

--- PAGE 33 ---
Inputs for Terrain Generator
● Feature Count – Records total instance count of detected terrain objects per 
standardized area.
● Feature Size – Classifies dimension metrics derived from camera distance 
and 3D positioning.
● Spatial Distribution – Maps relative distances and spatial arrangement 
between surface entities.


--- PAGE 34 ---
Proposed Approach
● 2D Feature Representation – For each image, we generate a representative 
2D map of how all features are oriented in space.
● Neural Associations – We could use a neural network or an encoder model to 
learn associations between various features and generate new maps.

--- PAGE 35 ---
Ideas for Generating New Terrain Based On Data
1) Train a diffusion model on lunar feature maps to learn the spatial patterns of 
craters, rocks, slopes, and surface roughness. The model will generate 
multiple realistic terrain-map variations while preserving the overall structure 
of the original map.
2) Represent lunar terrain features as discrete tokens and train a 
Transformer-based encoder-decoder model to learn how those features are 
spatially related. The model would then generate new terrain maps by 
predicting plausible feature arrangements based on patterns learned from the 
original maps.
FEEDBACK REQUEST: Do either of these ideas seem plausible, or would you 
prefer us to follow a different approach to generate terrain from our known data?
