1. Introduction 
1.1 Purpose 
 
The Data Products and Archive software interface specifications describes the data 
products, format and content of the Navigational Camera (NavCam) Planetary Data 
System (PDS) data archive  bundles in which data products  with associated meta 
data, documentation, and supporting materials are stored.  This document is 
intended to provide enough information to enable users to read and understand 
the NavCam data products stored in PDS. 
1.2 Scope 
 
The specifications in this document apply to all NavCam data products submitted 
for archival to the ISRO Science Data Archive (ISDA), for all the mission phases of 
the Chandrayaan-3. 
1.3  Data Products and Archive SIS Content Overview 
 
Section 2 of this document describes the Mission overview  while Section 3 
provides the characteristics of the Navigational Camera (NavCam). Section 4 gives 
an overview of data organization and data flow  while Section 5 describes data 
archive generation, delivery, and validation. Section 6  describes the archive 
structure and arch ive production responsibilities. Section 7 describes the file 
formats used in the archive, including the data product record structures. 
Individuals involved with generating the archive volumes are listed in Appendix-A 
While Appendix-B contains sample bundle label, Appendix-C, contains sample 
calibration collection label  Appendix-D contains sample data product label , and 
Appendix-E contain visualization of PDS4 data products. 
1.4  Audience 
 
This document serves both as a SIS and Interface Control Document (ICD). It 
describes both the archiving procedure and responsibilities, and data archive 
conventions and format. It is designed to be used both by the instrument data 
processing team in generating the archive, and by those wishing to unders tand 
the format and content of the NavCam PDS data product archive collection. 
Typically, these individuals would include scientists, data analysts, and software 
engineers.

2. Mission Overview 
 
Chandrayaan-3 is the third spacecraft in its series of Lunar exploration. It carries 
Propulsion Module (PM), Lander Module (LM) and Rover. Chandrayaan -3 composite 
module mass of 3900 kg, got injected into the Earth Parking Orbit (EPO) of 170 x 36505 
(nominal) dispersion ±500 km with command cut -off. In EPO, several Earth -Bound 
Maneuvers (EBN) were performed to increase the apogee of the orbit and the 
combined stack is injected into the Lunar Transfer Trajectory (LTT).  
 
The Propulsion Module carried  the combined stack to moon through a Lunar Orbit 
Insertion (LOI) maneuver. It is shown pictorially in Figure1. Subsequently, the 
combined stack was lowered into a lunar orbit of 125 km x 160km with Lunar Burns 
(LBNs). 
 
The Chandrayaan-3 mission carried PM & LM as composite until reaching Moon Bound 
Orbit of 125 km x 160km. Further, Propulsion Module and Lander module got 
separated as two independent satellites at pre -defined time and remain ed in same 
orbit for about one day. Further a de -boost maneuver was performed on lander to 
attain a orbit o f 100 km x 30 km. Lander remained  in this orbit for ~6 days to verify 
and validate the performance of all sub-systems followed by powered descent.  
 
The total maneuver strategy was designed such that the orbit plane contains  the 
desired landing site at powered descent start. At Perilune point, the powered descent 
got initiated through an Autonomous Landing Sequencer (ALS) and achieved a soft 
landing on the specified landing site located near lunar southern polar region. The 
mission profile is shown in Figure-1.  
 
2.1 Mission Objective 
 
 Expanding the technologies inherited fro m Chandrayaan -2 spacecraft and 
Develop and Demonstrate’ newer technologies that will be useful for future 
planetary missions. 
 
 To design, realize and deploy Lander capable of soft landing on a specified lunar 
site and deploy a Rover to carry out in-situ analysis of chemicals.

2.2  Payload Objective 
 
The Indian Rover landed on Moon through Chandrayaan-3 was a simple system to have 
the basic mobility and scientific operations on the lunar soil. The mobility of the Rover 
was planned to be semi -autonomous. A pair of Navigation Sensors was required to 
provide the imagery of the terrain ahead and a ground based image analysis and path 
planning system used these images to construct a three dimensional digital elevation 
model of the surface  features imaged. The useful stereo based DEM generation and 
photogrammetric depth  measurement demands that the field of views of both 
Navigation Sensors must overlap on each  other for more than 60%. Hence the 
Navigation Sensors are to be oriented on the Rover deck in such a way that the above 
requirements are met. NAV Sensor has to detect 20mm object from 5-meter distance 
and power requirement should be less than 2 watts.

3.2  Exposure Settings 
 
The NavCam datasets were acquired by level-0 during the RPP operations at MOX. For 
every mobility data sets were acquired with gain 2 and three exposure settings. Three 
exposure settings were set in terms of integration lines:  
 
 To avoid the getting dark image or oversaturated images.  
 Due to terrain difference and presence of obstacle, local surface normal will be 
different which will make integration time setting as terrain specific.  
Based on the sun illumination condition and NavCam viewing angle, the corresponding 
gain and exposure settings were selected and data was processed. The NavCams (left 
and right camera)  were operated with gain as 2. During the initial days different 
exposure settings were attempted and later go t finalized.  The finalized expo sure 
setting for the left image were the nominal integration line = 25 (25*0.35 = 8.75 msec) 
and for the right image, the nominal int egration line = 27 (27*0.35 = 9.45 msec). 
Integration time is expressed as integration line which is con figurable parameter got 
set during payload data acquisition. To derived the integration time in  msec, 
integration line needs to be multiplied with 0.35 factor. Further more details about the 
gain and exposure settings done throughout the mission is tabulated under Appendix

4.2  Data Products 
 
A PDS product consists of one or more digital and/or non -digital objects, and an 
accompanying PDS label file. Labeled digital objects are data products (i.e. 
electronically stored files). Labeled non -digital objects are physical and conceptual 
entities which have been described by a PDS label. PDS labels provide identification 
and description information for labeled objects. The PDS label defines a Logical 
Identifier (LID) by which any PDS labeled product is referenced throughout the system. 
In PDS4 labels are XML formatted ASCII files. More information on the formatting of 
PDS labels is provided in Section 6.3. More information on the usage of LIDs and the 
formation of Chandrayaan-3 LIDs is provided in Section 6 .1. The PDS4 Data Products 
for NavCam defined based on the defi ned data processing levels (L0 and L1) as Raw  
and Calibrated data products. Table 6 shows the data products definitions for each

5. Archive Generation 
 
The NavCam archive products are produced by the SAC DP team at SAC POC. The 
archive volume creation process  will be described in this section . Archived data 
established at ISSDC by transferring the LTA data sets from the SAC POC, are made 
available to users electronically as soon as it qualifies as a science product but not 
later than lock in period after the delivery and validation of the data. 
5.1 Data Processing and Production Software 
 
After receiving Level 0 data from the level-0 software at SAC POC via NKN, SAC DP 
Software will take level-0 data as input and generate Level 0/1 raw and calibrated 
science data products. The science data products that the SAC DP Software delivers 
to the ISSDC will be stored by the ISSDC and will be made available to the PI team

science data products to the ISDA for distribution to the public and long -term 
archiving in accordance with the NavCam Data Products  & Archive SIS (t his

5.2 Data Validation 
 
There are two types of data validation. The first is validation of the science data 
and the second one is validation of the compliance of the archive with PDS 
archiving and distribution requirements. The first type of validation is carried  out 
by the PI Team, and the second is overseen by the ISDA, in coordination with the 
PI Team. The formal validation of data content, adequacy of documentation, and 
adherence to PDS archiving and distribution requirements is scheduled and 
coordinated by th e ISDA. This review include s peer review from the science 
community, at the ISDA’s discretion. The review process may result in “liens,” which 
are actions that are recommended by the reviewers or by PDS personnel to correct 
the archive. All liens must be resolved by the data-set provider. Once the liens are 
cleared, the ISDA completes a final validation before release.

7.3 PDS Labels 
PDS labels are ASCII text files written, in the eXtensible Markup Language (XML). All 
product labels are detached from the digital files (if any) containing the data objects 
they de scribe (except Product_Bundle) . There is one label for every product. Each 
product, however, may contain one more data objects. The data objects of a given 
product may all reside in a single file, or they may be stored in multiple separate files. 
PDS4 label files must end with the file extension “.xml”. 
7.3.1 XML Documents

schema based upon the 1.11.0.0 version of the PDS Information Model for structure, 
and the   1.11.0.0 version of the PDS schematron for content. By use of an XML editor

Appendix-G: Raw to Calibrated Data Conversion 
 
The step wise procedure for converting raw image data to calibrated image data image 
raw count to rad cunt is as follows: 
 
(1) Raw Count Data Extraction - Read the raw data (left camera or right camera) 
get the raw count data. Subtract the offset from the raw count data using 
following formula. 
Raw count = Raw Image Count - offset 
Where, 
           Raw Image Count is raw image pixel value 
           Offset is Look up table  under calibration directory (table 17) based on 
the left or right camera data processing. 
 ch3_nav_left_offset.txt  
 ch3_nav_right_offset.txt 
(2) Get Raw Minimum and Maximum Pixel Value - After the subtraction (step 1), 
get the minimum and maximum count value stored as raw_min and raw_max. 
(3) LUT Application - Look up table is applied to the Raw Count data using 
following formula 
Radiance value     =   Raw count * multiplicative factor (gain) + gain_offset 
where, 
Raw Count is the raw image data values obtained from step (1), 
multiplicative factor (gain) is obtained from the Look up table based on left or 
right camera data processing, 
 ch3_nav_left_gain.txt  
 ch3_nav_right_gain.txt 
gain_offset is obtained from the Look up table based on the left or right camera 
data processing, 
 ch3_nav_left_gain_offset.txt 
 ch3_nav_right_gain_offset.txt 
(4) Get Rad Minimum and Maximum Pixel Value - after the LUT application (step 
3), get the minimum and maximum count value stored as rad_min and 
rad_max. 
(5) Radiance Data Generation – Final step the radiance data is generated using 
following formula 
Radiance value =   Radiance value * (raw_max - raw_ min)/ (rad_ max - 
rad_min) where, 
Radiance value is the rad image data values obtained from step (3), 
raw_ max is from step (2), raw_ min is from step (2) 
rad_ max is from step (4), rad_ min is from step (4)

Appendix-H: CAHV Camera Model 
 
The CAHV model is a linear, perspective -projection camera model (equivalent to a 
pinhole camera). It consists of four 3 -vectors (C,A,H,V) that describe the internal and 
external camera model parameters needed to translate between 2D image 
coordinates and 3D world coordinates. C (Vector_Center) is the 3D position of the 
pinhole (center of the e ntrance pupil). A (Vector_Axis) is a unit vector normal to the 
image plane pointing outward. H (Vector_Horizontal) is a composite vector encoding 
three quantities: H' (a vector in the image plane perpendicular to the vertical columns), 
Hs (the distance bet ween the lens center and image plane, measured in horizontal 
pixels), and Hc (the horizontal image coordinate directly under C when moving parallel 
to A). V (Vector_Vertical) similarly composites the analogous V', Vs, and Vc in the 
vertical direction. 
 
C - The 3D position of the entrance pupil. 
 
A - A unit vector normal to the image plane pointing outward (towards C). 
 
H - A vector pointing roughly rightward in the image; it is a composite of the 
orientation of the CCD rows, the horizontal scale, the horizontal center. 
 
V - A vector pointing roughly downward in the image; it is a composite of the 
orientation of the CCD columns, the vertical scale, the vertical center, and A. 
 
If P is a point in the scene, then the corresponding image locations x and y can be  
computed from: 
 
              (P-C) H 
x      = ------------- 
              (P-C) A 
 
              (P-C) V 
y       = ------------- 
              (P-C) A 
 
 
For details on the camera model math and calibration and more description of the 
CAHV-model family, see references [Ref 11].
